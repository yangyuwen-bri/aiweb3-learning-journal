## Learning Notes

Initial framing:

- Wallet and private key are about account control and personal sovereignty, not just login.
- Transaction and signature are the bridge between a user's intention and on-chain state changes.
- Blockchain network operation explains why transactions need broadcasting, validation, consensus, and confirmation.
- Smart contracts are executable logic on shared state, not ordinary backend functions.
- Protocol upgrades show that blockchains are not static; governance and compatibility matter.
- Web3 boundaries help identify what should be on-chain, what should stay off-chain, and what must remain human-confirmed.

Q&A added to today's note:

- Private key and personal sovereignty: the private key is the root control of a Web3 account; self-sovereignty means controlling identity and assets without relying completely on a platform, but also taking responsibility for key safety.
- Message signature vs transaction signature: message signatures usually prove identity or consent; transaction signatures can change on-chain state, such as transfers, approvals, contract writes, or deployments.
- Protocol upgrades: upgrades change shared blockchain rules and usually involve core developers, node operators, validators, infrastructure providers, applications, and community governance.
